## Summary

LinkedIn post by Daniel Lombraña González PhD citing research showing developers do not spend most of their time coding. The post references an ACM article ("Eight Myths on Software Engineering and GenAI") and challenges the assumption that software development is primarily a code-generation activity.

The framing directly counters the narrative that GenAI tools optimizing for code velocity represent a complete productivity gain. Instead, it emphasizes that developers spend significant time in collaborative, non-coding activities: meetings, standups, planning, code reviews, and knowledge-sharing.

The post received engagement from practitioners: a commenter (Pelayo Marinas Garcia) extended the argument to architects, noting that architectural work is also dominated by stakeholder collaboration, alignment, decision-making, and communication rather than design artifacts.

## Verbatim quotes

> "Software isn't written in a vacuum and, as such, developers need both to spend time independently writing code and in group activities such as meetings, standups, planning, and code reviews. Multiple research projects have looked at how developers spend their days and their time—and the results clearly show that developers don't spend most of their time coding."

> "I totally agree. The same applies to architects—we don't spend most of our time designing architectures. A huge part of the job is collaborating with stakeholders, aligning teams, reviewing designs, making decisions, documenting, and communicating." (Pelayo Marinas Garcia)

## Takeaways

- **Research-backed premise**: The post cites existing empirical work on developer time allocation, grounding the claim in evidence rather than anecdote.
- **GenAI productivity reframing**: Suggests that focusing GenAI purely on code generation misses the majority of developer work. Productivity gains require addressing collaboration and knowledge transfer.
- **Team-scale complexity**: The implicit model is that distributed cognition—planning, alignment, code review—becomes the binding constraint as teams grow.
- **Architectural implications**: Extended by practitioner comment to question whether GenAI can improve non-coding work; current tools optimize for the minority activity.
