## Summary

Chris Ford announced the early access release of his O'Reilly book *Agentic Engineering at Scale*, which addresses the gap between personal productivity with coding agents and enterprise-scale deployment. Ford frames the book as a response to a common frustration: weekend prototyping success that doesn't translate to reliable progress in production work environments.

The core premise is that real value from agentic systems comes not from agent capability alone, but from the surrounding engineering infrastructure. Ford originally proposed the title *Agentic Enterprise Architecture* but was advised by his editor that it sounded "too much of a downer" for market appeal.

The book is positioned to cover:
- Moving beyond individual productivity as the primary lens
- Agentic systems as part of integrated engineering architecture
- Reliable harnesses and guardrails (per commenter Ricardo Moura's interpretation)
- Intent clarity and complexity management at scale
- The balance between autonomy, reliability, and maintainability

The comment from Moura articulates a key framing: "the real differentiator will increasingly be less about which model teams use and more about how well the surrounding engineering system is designed."
